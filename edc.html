The Weekend

Three nights at Tinker Field

EDC Orlando is Insomniac's East Coast edition of Electric Daisy Carnival, and it runs November 6 to 8, 2026 at Tinker Field, the old ballpark grounds west of downtown. For one weekend the field becomes the biggest dance floor in Florida: multiple stages, carnival scale production, and a crowd that flies in for it.

Insomniac runs the festival through its brand arms, and Orlando feels both of them. Factory 93 is the house and techno arm and has run stage takeovers at EDC Orlando; Bassrush is the bass arm, behind the circuitGROUNDS stage identity. Which arm you follow decides which festival you get.

The basics

  • Gates run 1 PM to 12 AM all three days, rain or shine. The festival is 18+; alcohol and VIP areas are 21+.
  • Tinker Field, 287 S Tampa Ave, next to Camping World Stadium. This year's theme is kineticJOURNEY.
  • Five stages: kineticFIELD, circuitGROUNDS, neonGARDEN, stereoBLOOM and CASA BACARDÍ, with Factory 93, Bassrush and Insomniac Records takeovers across the weekend.
  • No re-entry, per day. Once you leave, you are out until tomorrow.
  • Box office and will call are off-site, at Lake Lorna Doone Park, corner of W Church St and Rio Grande Ave. Entry runs until one hour before close, with a ticket not yet scanned.
  • Set times are not out yet (checked October 20, 2026). They land in the Insomniac app in the final weeks; anyone selling a confirmed schedule today is guessing.

Headliners by day

  • Friday: David Guetta, Afrojack, Mau P, Levity, Wooli, Whethan, Chris Lorenzo, Interplanetary Criminal, Josh Baker b2b Prospa. Sunset sets: Alesso, Adventure Club, Brunello, MALUGI.
  • Saturday: Kaskade, Steve Aoki, Alison Wonderland, Ray Volpe, Nico Moreno, Dennis Cruz, ACRAZE b2b CID, Skull Machine. Sunset sets: Alan Walker, Devault, Rossi.
  • Sunday: Martin Garrix, Hardwell, Alok, Meduza, I Hate Models, Boys Noize b2b Brutalismus 3000, Deorro b2b DJ Diesel, TroyBoi, San Holo (Wholesome Riddim set). Sunset sets: SLANDER, Marlon Hoffstadt, Of The Trees.

At the gate

  • Entry is a TSA-style search: pockets emptied, bags opened, full pat-down.
  • Bags: clear only, max 12" x 6" x 12". Clutches up to 6" x 9", clear or not. Hydration packs with at most two main compartments plus one smaller one, empty on entry. Tinted, iridescent and opalescent bags are banned, even if see-through-ish.
  • Confiscated on sight: over-the-counter medication, eye drops, liquid makeup, aerosols including spray sunscreen. Cigarettes, gum, tampons and wipes must arrive sealed. Meds and eye drops are sold inside at the general store and first aid.
  • Prescriptions pass only unexpired, in the labeled bottle, matching your ID, in the amount needed for the day, after a consult with a safety officer. Sealed intranasal naloxone (Narcan) is explicitly allowed in.
  • Costumes cannot resemble public safety or event staff uniforms, hi-vis vests included. Totems cap at 10 ft tall with a pole no thicker than 1 inch and no solid metal; red light wands and LED gloves are banned.

ID and wristbands

  • Physical photo ID only: a US government license or ID, or a physical passport. No digital IDs. Expired IDs, school IDs, consular IDs and birth certificates are all rejected.
  • A foreign license needs a color photocopy of the passport alongside it, and alcohol and VIP entry still require the physical passport or a US ID.
  • Passes come from Front Gate Tickets only, limit four per person. Once a ticket is scanned it is non-transferable, and a 3-day pass scans once per day. International orders are Will Call only.
  • Under 21 holding a VIP pass? There is no refund; Front Gate or the box office will exchange it even for a GA pass.

The Roots

Here since 2011

Tinker Field has been EDC's Orlando home since the show first debuted here in 2011. The debut was a different scale of thing: about 12,000 people on the Friday and over 20,000 on the Saturday, with Paul van Dyk, Diplo and Benny Benassi headlining and two then rising names, Porter Robinson and Skrillex, lower on the bill. The festival that returns each November grew out of that weekend, in the city that had already given American dance music the Florida breaks. The longer story of that sound lives in the EDM section's history.

What to Avoid

The moves that wreck the weekend

Twenty-five ways this festival goes wrong, grouped the way you will meet them: the gate, the ride, the money, and the night itself. Each one is drawn from the promoter's own published rules, Florida statute, or reporting checked in October 2026.

First, know the help system

  • Ground Control is Insomniac's harm-reduction team: roaming crews in purple shirts carrying water and radios. Flag one down for water, directions, a lost friend, or a friend in trouble. The ask stays with them; there is no police involvement in asking for help.
  • First aid is free, no questions asked. Insomniac's own Health & Wellness page says so, and adds that seeking medical help will not get you in trouble. Medical stations are marked on the festival map.
  • Florida law backs that promise. Under § 893.21, a person acting in good faith who seeks medical help for someone experiencing (or believed to be experiencing) an alcohol or drug overdose may not be arrested, charged, prosecuted or penalized for possession or paraphernalia, when the evidence came from seeking that help. The same protection covers the person in danger. A companion statute, § 562.112, gives a similar protection when the emergency involves alcohol provided to someone under 21, provided you stay at the scene until EMS arrives and cooperate. The limits, plainly: the immunity covers possession and paraphernalia only. It does not cover trafficking, sale or distribution.
  • Sealed intranasal naloxone (Narcan) is on the official allowed list. Carry it in.
  • There is no drug-checking service inside, and no amnesty box at the gates. The gate is a search, not a surrender bin. Plan on that basis.

The enforcement reality, in the promoter's own words. Insomniac's festival guidelines state: "Police officers work both inside and outside our events. All narcotics laws are strictly enforced. Narcotics officers will be working both areas," under a zero-tolerance drug policy. That posture is not theoretical in this city: in October 2026, weeks before this festival, an OPD six-week undercover operation bought methamphetamine, cocaine, MDMA and cannabis at 9 downtown clubs, including Elixir and Celine, two of the official afterparty rooms named on this page. Fourteen people were arrested for selling to undercover officers, with 35 drug arrests across the operation (FOX 35 Orlando; Orlando Shine, October 2026). Florida exposure is severe: MDMA possession is a third-degree felony under § 893.13(6)(a), up to 5 years in prison and a $5,000 fine, plus a driver's license suspension; at 10 grams or more of total mixture weight it becomes trafficking under § 893.135(1)(k), a first-degree felony with a 3-year mandatory minimum and a $50,000 fine. And the Florida Department of Health's standing warning is blunt: any pill, liquid or powder you did not get directly from a pharmacy may contain fentanyl, undetectable by sight, taste or smell.

Getting in

10. Bringing the medicine cabinet

The move to avoid: Packing over-the-counter meds, eye drops, spray sunscreen, liquid makeup, or an open pack of gum or cigarettes.

The false assumption: "Security waves through normal stuff."

What actually happens: The published list bans OTC medication, eye drops, aerosols and liquid makeup outright, and requires cigarettes, gum, tampons and wipes sealed at entry. Confiscated is confiscated; the gate does not store things. Prescriptions survive only labeled, ID-matching, day-quantity, after a safety-officer consult.

What to do instead: Lotion sunscreen, powder makeup, sealed everything, and buy eye drops or painkillers at the inside general store or first aid.

What it costs: A few dollars inside; your actual prescription routine disrupted if you pack it wrong.

11. The bag that dies at the gate

The move to avoid: A normal backpack, a tinted "clear-ish" bag, or a hydration pack with three main compartments.

The false assumption: "Clear bag policy means any clear bag."

What actually happens: Specs are exact: clear max 12" x 6" x 12", clutch max 6" x 9", hydration packs max two main compartments plus one small one, empty. Tinted and iridescent are banned by name. Failing bags get turned away with no re-entry safety net and no bag check outside.

What to do instead: Buy the spec bag before Orlando (they cost little online), or go clutch-only plus a compliant hydration pack.

What it costs: About $10 to $15 for a compliant bag at home; a trip back to the car or hotel at the gate.

12. Forgetting that there is no re-entry

The move to avoid: Leaving anything essential in the car or hotel "to grab later."

The false assumption: "Festivals let you pop out."

What actually happens: No re-entry, per day, no exceptions published. The jacket, medication, ID or charger you left behind is gone until tomorrow. Combined with a 1 PM gate, that is an 11-hour commitment to whatever you carried in.

What to do instead: Run the gate checklist before entry: ID, wristband, phone, power bank, sealed essentials, a layer for after dark, empty bottle.

What it costs: One minute of checking; an 11-hour day missing the thing you needed otherwise.

15. Buying wristbands from resellers, socials, or a guy in the lot

The move to avoid: Any pass not bought from Front Gate (or a genuinely verified transfer).

The false assumption: "A wristband is a wristband."

What actually happens: The official ticketing FAQ is blunt: buy only from Front Gate Tickets, because an illegitimate or scalped ticket means turned away at the gate with no refund, and once a ticket is scanned it is non-transferable. A band someone else already used, or a screenshot of a code, is worthless. Counterfeit and already-scanned passes are the standard festival scam, discovered at the gate with no recourse.

What to do instead: Front Gate only; if the tier you want is gone, buy the tier that exists. For venue afterparties, Eventbrite and the venue's authorized resale (Tixel at The Vanguard) only.

What it costs: Face value plus fees; the scam version costs the whole weekend at the gate.

20. Costume and totem surprises

The move to avoid: A police, security or medic-look costume, a gas mask, LED gloves for light shows, or a heroic totem build.

The false assumption: "It's a rave; self-expression has no rules."

What actually happens: Uniform-resembling costumes (including hi-vis vests), gas masks, LED gloves and microlights, and red light wands are prohibited by name. Totems cap at 10 ft, pole max 1 inch diameter, no solid metal, inflatables deflated at entry. Gate staff decide borderline calls, and their call is final.

What to do instead: Build inside the published spec sheet on the official Hours & Info page; pool-noodle totems are the officially suggested hack.

What it costs: A costume redesign at home; confiscation or a turned-away entry at the gate.

Getting there and back

3. Driving in cold and hunting for parking

The move to avoid: Driving to Tinker Field without a prepaid ParkMobile pass and "finding something nearby."

The false assumption: "It's a stadium; stadiums have parking."

What actually happens: The official parking page calls General Parking "Extremely Limited" and steers drivers to a Downtown Parking & Shuttle service instead; Premier Parking is already sold out for 2026, lots open at 10 AM, there is no tailgating, and cars left past 2 AM get towed. The 2026 general price was not published on the official page when this was checked on October 20, 2026; 2025's was $40 a day. The fallback is unofficial yard parking in the Parramore and West Lakes blocks: cash, unlit, unsecured, and the walk back at 1 AM is its own problem.

What to do instead: Prepay official parking the moment it is bookable if you must drive, or take the $123.59 3-day shuttle and delete the problem.

What it costs: Roughly $120 for three days at last year's rate if you land official parking; an unlit yard, a possible break-in, a tow, or no spot at all if bought wrong.

4. Building the ride home on SunRail or the bus

The move to avoid: Planning to "just take the train or bus back" at midnight.

The false assumption: "A city this size runs event transit."

What actually happens: SunRail is weekday-only and even its latest event-night patterns end by about 11 PM; no EDC service exists, and there is no weekend service at all. The city's own EDC travel page says SunRail does not run for this festival. LYMMO stops around 10 to 10:30 PM; stadium LYNX routes mostly end between 7:25 PM (Sunday) and about 10:25 PM, with a single late Link 21 toward downtown around 1:20 AM on Saturday as the exception, and all of them detour around stadium road closures on event nights. Thousands of first-timers discover the general shape of this at 12:15 AM in the rideshare lot.

What to do instead: Shuttle pass, prepaid parking, or a rideshare plan built on the official Lot 10 pickup, with patience budgeted.

What it costs: Nothing extra if planned; surge pricing and a long, sketchy wait if discovered live.

5. The midnight rideshare scrum, unprepared

The move to avoid: Exiting with a reported ~100,000 people, opening Uber at 8% battery, and requesting from the curb.

The false assumption: "Surge is just a price; cars will come."

What actually happens: Every attendee, driver, shuttle and car converges on the same few streets at 12 AM. Data throttles, ETAs lie, drivers cancel in gridlock, and surge multiplies. A dedicated EDC parking guide (edcorlandoparking.com) warns the designated zone at end of night will be "extremely busy" with "high surge pricing," and that street pickups away from the zone "can be dangerous, illegal, and will likely be prevented by road closures and law enforcement." The designated rideshare zone sits in the Tampa Ave and Long St area by the stadium lots; the exact lot assignment changes by event, so follow the event guide and signage instead of inventing a corner. No concrete 2026 surge fare is documented anywhere, so treat specific dollar figures circulating online as anecdote.

What to do instead: Charge the phone (locker charging exists), request from the official lot, walk a few blocks east toward downtown before requesting if the lot is jammed, or leave 15 minutes before the headliner ends, or wait 30 minutes after and let the wave pass.

What it costs: 30 to 60 minutes and normal-ish pricing if played patiently; triple-digit surge and a dead phone if not.

6. Wandering off west, wasted, at 1 AM

The move to avoid: Letting the most intoxicated member of the group walk alone into the blocks west or northwest of the stadium, or doing it yourself.

The false assumption: "The stadium's in downtown; everywhere around it is downtown."

What actually happens: Downtown is east. West and northwest are Parramore and West Lakes residential streets and the Orange Blossom Trail corridor: dark, unfamiliar, and you are visibly impaired, phoneless or lost, at the exact hour event crowds are watched. Calibrated honestly: this research found no news reports of attacks on EDC walkers; the risk is exposure and vulnerability, not a documented attack pattern.

What to do instead: The group stays together; walk east on Church St toward downtown lights if walking at all; use the shuttle or the Lot 10 rideshare; the friend who cannot walk safely gets Ground Control or the medics inside the fence, not a solo sidewalk exit.

What it costs: Ten minutes of herding buys the safe version; the unsafe version risks everything a vulnerable, incapacitated person risks on an unfamiliar street at 1 AM.

7. Leaving valuables in the car, anywhere near the stadium

The move to avoid: Bags, laptops, merch, or anything visible on the seats, in any lot, official or yard.

The false assumption: "It's an event lot; everyone's at a festival."

What actually happens: Vehicle break-ins are the recurring property crime around stadium events, and EDC's own parking page warns attendees not to leave valuables in the car while disclaiming liability for theft from vehicles. A car full of visible weekend gear, parked for 11 hours in a known event zone, is the softest target in the neighborhood that night.

What to do instead: Everything in the trunk before you arrive (not after you park), nothing visible in the cabin, an official lit lot over a stranger's yard.

What it costs: Thirty seconds at home packing the trunk; a smashed window and a ruined Sunday otherwise.

18. "Downtown is a five-minute stroll"

The move to avoid: Drifting out of the gates on foot with no direction, especially as a group that splits.

The false assumption: "The stadium is downtown, so any walk leads somewhere good."

What actually happens: It is about 2 miles east to downtown's core, and 1.5 to 1.9 miles to the downtown hotels: 20 to 30 minutes on foot, under I-4, at midnight, in a crowd that thins fast, with Church Street beside the stadium closed to cars for construction. Half the compass points lead into residential blocks instead. Groups split at exactly the wrong moment.

What to do instead: Decide the exit plan before the last set: shuttle gate, Lot 10, or walk east on Church St together, phones alive.

What it costs: A plan made at 11 PM saves the 1 AM version of the problem.

21. Leaving at the stroke of midnight with everyone

The move to avoid: Joining the full exit surge, a reported ~100,000-person day crowd, to the same shuttles, lots and rideshare curb.

The false assumption: "Leaving at close is leaving."

What actually happens: Midnight concentrates the entire day's crowd into the same few streets feeding I-4 and SR-408 and the shuttle loops; lines and ETAs peak exactly then.

What to do instead: Leave 15 minutes before the final set ends, or stay in for the last song and deliberately wait out the first wave inside the glow (water, sit, charge), then move.

What it costs: The last 15 minutes of one set, or 30 calm minutes, against an hour of surge.

23. Trusting a third-party party bus with your ride home

The move to avoid: Booking an unofficial party bus or promoter shuttle because it was cheaper than the official pass.

The false assumption: "A bus is a bus."

What actually happens: Third-party buses have no dedicated routes or drop zones, sit in the same gridlock as cars, and the independent parking and transport guides for this festival carry a standing warning: mixed reviews, late departures, and attendees stranded. The official program's buses load at the gates on festival-controlled loops.

What to do instead: Official shuttle pass, or a hotel on an official line (the Hotel EDC Shuttle serves the Kissimmee resort; the Green and Blue lines serve I-Drive). If you do use a third party, vet it like a vendor, not a vibe. And guard the official pass itself: as of 2026, lost shuttle passes are not replaced.

What it costs: $123.59 for the guaranteed version; the cheap version can cost the ride home.

Money

2. The shuttle sticker shock

The move to avoid: Discovering the shuttle price at checkout.

The false assumption: "A festival this size must run free shuttles," or "the shuttle is a $20 add-on."

What actually happens: The official Insomniac shuttle runs six lines to the stadium, and it is the single best logistics buy of the weekend, but it costs $123.59 for a 3-day pass at the current Tier 1 (about $41 a day) or $52.88 for one day, and tiers rise as they sell. Buying late is the "gotcha": same buses, higher tier.

What to do instead: Buy the 3-day pass at Tier 1 when you buy your ticket. If you are in one day only, weigh the $52.88 single-day pass against surge-priced midnight rideshare, which usually loses.

What it costs: $123.59 bought early; more later, per person.

8. Staying on I-Drive or out by UCF with no plan

The move to avoid: Booking the cheap room wherever it is and assuming the trip is a detail.

The false assumption: "Orlando hotels are all near everything."

What actually happens: I-Drive sits 5.9 miles from Tinker Field; UCF is clear across the metro; Kissimmee is about 22 miles away. The official shuttle map is the tell: the Orange and Yellow lines serve the Universal area, the Green and Blue lines serve I-Drive, the Silver line serves Lake Buena Vista by Disney, and the Hotel EDC Shuttle serves the Kissimmee resort. Downtown hotels, 1.5 to 1.9 miles east of the gates and walkable in 20 to 30 minutes, are NOT on a shuttle line at all, because they do not need to be. Every night ends with the same 12 AM math: distance times surge.

What to do instead: Price the hotel by the ride home, not the nightly rate: downtown east for walkability and afterparties, or a hotel on one of the six shuttle lines (see Going, below).

What it costs: A higher nightly rate downtown, or $123.59 in shuttle passes on a line hotel; the "cheap" room plus three midnight surges costs more.

16. Waiting to buy until the tier is gone

The move to avoid: "Tickets will be there in October."

The false assumption: "Festival tickets don't really sell out here."

What actually happens: Prices checked at Front Gate on October 20, 2026: 3-day GA $336.99 all-in, GA+ $379.99, VIP $659.66; 1-day GA $149.22, with Friday single-day GA already sold out. Front Gate also runs a layaway plan, so the pass does not have to land in one payment. At the gate, what remains is the secondary market at a markup, or the scam market (see item 15).

What to do instead: Buy the tier you actually want when it is on sale; use layaway if cash flow is the blocker.

What it costs: $336.99 for 3-day GA at current pricing; more, or a fake, if you wait.

17. Checking into Hotel EDC and assuming the ride is covered

The move to avoid: Booking the official hotel for the convenience and not reading the inclusions list.

The false assumption: "Official hotel means transport to the festival is handled."

What actually happens: Hotel EDC is the first East Coast edition of the format, at Margaritaville Resort Orlando in Kissimmee, 17 to 22 miles and roughly 30 to 40 minutes from Tinker Field, Vibee-run, with four nights of on-site parties. The festival shuttle from the resort is an $89 add-on, not included. Non-guests can attend the parties only on a 4-day party pass ($719.28 GA, $1,149.77 VIP, checked October 2026).

What to do instead: If you book it, add the $89 shuttle at booking and treat the on-site parties (11 PM to 6 AM, four nights) as the value, not the location.

What it costs: Room plus passes plus $89 a head for the shuttle; forgetting it costs three nights of Kissimmee-to-stadium surge.

22. Booking the all-inclusive bundle blind

The move to avoid: Buying a ticket-plus-hotel-plus-shuttle bundle because the checkout page bundles it.

The false assumption: "Bundled means discounted."

What actually happens: The official Vibee packages exist and are legitimate, but bundling hides line items, and the shuttle question (is transport to the festival actually included, or an $89 add-on?) is exactly the kind that disappears inside a bundle total.

What to do instead: Price the same room and passes unbundled, line by line, then buy whichever is genuinely cheaper with inclusions you can name.

What it costs: Ten minutes of comparison; bundles bought blind cost whatever the bundle felt like charging.

Staying whole

1. Believing "it's a drug-free festival" covers you

The move to avoid: Assuming possession is decriminalized, tolerated, or "just part of the scene," and carrying accordingly.

The false assumption: "Nobody actually gets arrested for personal amounts at a rave."

What actually happens: See the enforcement block above: the promoter's own zero-tolerance notice, and an October 2026 undercover operation that netted 14 seller arrests at 9 downtown clubs including two of this festival's official afterparty rooms. In Florida, MDMA possession is a third-degree felony; 10 grams of mixture or more is trafficking with a 3-year mandatory minimum. The state health department separately warns that any pill or powder not from a pharmacy may contain fentanyl.

What to do instead: The clean answer: do not carry. If anyone in your orbit is in medical trouble, use the help system at the top of this section; § 893.21 exists precisely so that calling for help is not itself a crime scene.

What it costs: A felony record, license suspension, and up to 5 years for simple possession; the mandatory-minimum version starts at $50,000 and 3 years.

9. The phone that never comes home

The move to avoid: Phone loose in an open back pocket or an unzippered bag, in a dense crowd, all weekend.

The false assumption: "Pickpockets are a European festival problem."

What actually happens: Organized theft crews work big US festivals, and attendee-reported phone and bag theft at this one runs in pattern with the rest of the circuit; EDM Identity warned ahead of the 2025 edition about pickpocketing and bag-slashing tactics at the festival. What is not published is a verified local count for any year, so treat specific numbers circulating online as unproven. The aftermath is the real cost either way: no phone means no rideshare, no map, no meeting point, no way home at 1 AM.

What to do instead: Front pocket or a zipped, body-side pouch; a lanyard or tether inside the crowd; a festival locker with a built-in charger doubles as the phone plan (reported around $15 a day). Share live location with your group before the gates.

What it costs: A $10 tether and locker money against losing the device your entire exit plan lives on.

13. Paying for bottled water beside a free water station

The move to avoid: Buying bottle after bottle all day because you never found the refill stations.

The false assumption: "Festival water is a vendor product."

What actually happens: Free water refill stations are a published festival feature; look for the droplet symbol on the official map. The official FAQ lists $3.50 bottled water as the alternative, and single water prices of $8 or more were reported by attendees at the 2024 edition. Over an 11-hour day in Florida heat, the bought-bottle route adds up fast.

What to do instead: Bring the empty bottle or compliant hydration pack, refill free all day, and treat bottled water as a backup.

What it costs: $0 with the station habit; a steady drip of $3.50 to $8 bottles without it.

14. Getting separated in a 100,000-person phone dead zone

The move to avoid: "Text me when you get there" as the whole plan.

The false assumption: "Cell service works; we'll just find each other."

What actually happens: Data throttles hard at peak load inside a six-figure crowd, batteries die on full-brightness screens by 10 PM, and free Wi-Fi is a VIP perk, not a public utility. A reported ~100,000-person day turns "meet by the stage" into a needle search, and EDC's crowd is a costume crowd: your friend is one of nine thousand people in neon.

What to do instead: Insomniac builds numbered lighting pillars around the grounds as meeting points; pick a pillar, a stage-side landmark, and a time, out loud, before gates. Download the map, set times and your hotel address offline. Screenshot your hotel address for the driver.

What it costs: Two minutes of planning; a separated night costs hours and possibly the whole group's evening.

19. Underestimating Florida heat at a "night" festival

The move to avoid: Treating the afternoon like a warm-up act: no water plan, no shade plan, alcohol early.

The false assumption: "EDC is a nighttime thing; heat is a daytime-festival problem."

What actually happens: Gates open at 1 PM. The afternoon is when heat and dehydration land, and they are the main medical driver at an event like this in Orlando in November, when afternoons still run warm and humid. Dehydration plus stimulants plus dancing is the classic path to the medical tent, and it starts hours before sunset.

What to do instead: Electrolytes before noon, a refill rhythm from the free stations (not thirst-driven), shade breaks between sets, and pace the drinking until dark.

What it costs: Free water and discipline; the medical tent costs the night, minimum.

24. Doing the whole weekend on festival time

The move to avoid: 1 PM to 12 AM at the festival and 12 AM to 6 AM at an afterparty, three nights running, on no sleep and no food plan.

The false assumption: "Sleep is for Monday."

What actually happens: The official ecosystem now runs around the clock by design: Elixir's afterparties, Hotel EDC's 11 PM to 6 AM parties, Mango's bills to 6 AM. Sleep deprivation compounds everything else on this list: judgment, heat tolerance, phone discipline, the walk home.

What to do instead: Pick your nights. One full send, one early exit, real sleep between, a real meal before gates (food inside runs a reported $14 to $20 a plate).

What it costs: Missing one sunrise set against arriving at Sunday intact.

25. Assume the Florida sky will hold

The move to avoid: Dressing for a dry desert night and treating weather as somebody else's problem.

The false assumption: "EDC is a desert festival; rain is not in the script."

What actually happens: This is Orlando in November, the wet tail of hurricane season. In 2025 the Sunday show was shut down for about an hour by heavy rain and lightning, and the grounds turned to mud with crowd-flow bottlenecks as people funneled to shelter. In 2022, Hurricane Nicole passed over Central Florida just days before the festival. Typical November normals run near 78°F by day and the high 50s at night, with the rain risk front-loaded in the afternoon.

What to do instead: A cheap poncho is the rain gear the gate allows (umbrellas must be 10 inches folded or under); shoes you can sacrifice to mud; a waterproof pouch for the phone; and when lightning pauses the show, move to shelter early instead of joining the bottleneck.

What it costs: A $2 poncho and one pair of beaters; the unprepared version costs the Sunday set and a soaked walk home.

Every one of these checks out against the sources linked from the guide's research files, and the short version is the same advice Orlando gives all weekend: decide the ride home before the first set, keep the phone alive, use the free water, and let the help system help.

Practical Notes

Going

Plan the whole week, not just the weekend: the official parties start before the festival gates and the downtown rooms stay on festival time throughout. The events calendar carries EDC week with dates and sources, and the EDM section maps the rooms themselves. Festival dates and party bills are checked against official sources and can change; confirm before you go.

The ride, planned out

Official shuttles

Six lines run to a drop point by the stadium: Orange from Fairfield Inn & Suites near Universal, Green from Four Points by Sheraton I-Drive, Yellow from Comfort Inn & Suites near Universal, Blue from Embassy Suites I-Drive at ICON Park, Silver from Courtyard Lake Buena Vista at Vista Centre, plus the Hotel EDC Shuttle from Margaritaville Resort Orlando (non-guests may ride). Passes run $123.59 for 3 days or $52.88 for 1 day at Tier 1. Buses leave the hotels 12 PM to 8 PM and run back until an hour after the music ends; the drop-off is still about a 15-minute walk to the main entrance. New for 2026: a lost shuttle pass is not replaced.

Parking, honestly

The official parking page calls General Parking "Extremely Limited" and steers drivers to its Downtown Parking & Shuttle service; Premier Parking is sold out for 2026. Lots open at 10 AM, there is no tailgating, and cars left after 2 AM are towed. The 2026 general price had not been published when this page was checked (2025 was $40 a day). EDC's own warning: do not leave valuables in the car.

Rideshare and taxis

The designated rideshare zone is Lot 10, at Tampa Ave and Long St. At midnight it is the busiest few acres in Orlando: charge your phone, request from the lot, and let the surge pass or walk a few blocks east toward downtown before requesting. Street-corner pickups of your own invention get stopped by road closures and enforcement.

Transit truth

SunRail is weekday-only and runs no EDC service; its last trains leave downtown around 9:55 PM, so it cannot get you home. LYNX Links 20, 21, 36 and 107 serve the stadium area but detour on event nights and mostly end between 7:25 PM and about 10:25 PM, with one late Link 21 toward downtown around 1:20 AM on Saturday. LYMMO's downtown circulator ends about 10 to 10:30 PM. Transit is a way in, not a way home.

Where to base

Downtown east of the stadium is 1.5 to 1.9 miles from the gates, a 20 to 30 minute walk, and holds the afterparty rooms; no official shuttle line stops there, because it does not need one. I-Drive (5.9 miles) rides the Green and Blue lines, the Universal area rides Orange and Yellow, and Kissimmee, about 22 miles out, is the Hotel EDC Shuttle's line. Price every hotel by the ride home, not the nightly rate. Note: Church St beside the stadium is closed to cars for the stadium modernization, which runs to summer 2027.

Inside the gates

Free water refill stations are marked with a droplet symbol on the festival map; bottled water runs $3.50 by the official FAQ. Food runs a reported $14 to $20 a plate (2025 attendee reporting). Festival lockers have built-in phone chargers; free Wi-Fi is a VIP-only perk and data throttles at peak, so download the map, set times and your hotel address offline before you go, and agree on one of Insomniac's numbered lighting pillars as your meeting point.
