Lake Nona

The city of the future, already open: a lake made for playing, food halls in shipping containers, and fine dining with theater.

Scroll the story

Most planned communities feel like a spreadsheet learned to wear a polo. Lake Nona feels like the future snuck in early and brought its appetite. A medical city grew up around the lakes, then the chefs, artists, and wakeboarders moved in, and suddenly the newest corner of Orlando is the one with the shipping container food hall, the sculpture garden, and a lake built purely for playing on. Come see what tomorrow tastes like.

The Icons

The three reasons Lake Nona is a destination, not a suburb.

Park·$$

Boxi Park Lake Nona

Someone looked at forty-foot shipping containers and saw Lake Nona's best party, and they were right. Boxi Park stacks the boxes into a one-of-a-kind open-air hangout where you can graze lobster rolls from Claw & Order, tacos from Canonita, and housemade burgers while a live band plays and the dog races around the pup park. The lineup never sits still, with rotating pop-ups under the Before It's Gone banner, beach volleyball, and drinks from Bar Nona and Hops and Vine. Bring everyone.

Attraction·$$

Nona Adventure Park

This is where Lake Nona goes to play hooky. Nona Adventure Park turns a lake into a full-contact playground: a sprawling inflatable aqua park you bounce, climb, and wipe out across, a cable park for wakeboarders of every level, and a sixty-foot climbing tower with ropes courses and rock walls for the vertically ambitious. Refuel at Splashes Bar & Grill, catch the sunset over the water, and come back for karaoke nights and live music. Few places in Orlando wear you out this happily.

See it in the guide →

Latin·$$$$

Bacán

Say the name and watch Lake Nona sit up straighter. BACÁN is an immersive affair built around a dramatic open theater kitchen, where Chef Guillaume Robin, honored as a Maître Cuisinier de France, and Sous Chef Ryne Wood turn the boldly seductive flavors of the Americas into edible theater. Order the whole Florida snapper, save room for the banana tart finished with gold leaf, and watch a chocolate sphere melt open at your table. Elevated yet approachable, innovative and just a little bit unexpected, this is a night out that alters your perception.

Neighborhood Tables

The Tables

Beyond the icons: the neighborhood restaurants the locals actually eat at.

Tapas & Small Plates·$$

Chroma Modern Bar + Kitchen

Chroma is the Town Center's extrovert: a color splashed room built entirely around sharing. Order like you mean it. Whipped ricotta crostini, sliders, and the truffle fettuccine alfredo a server finishes tableside, tossing it inside a wheel of Parmesan while the table goes quiet. Weekends belong to the molten strawberry french toast and a round of espresso martinis. Nobody orders just one plate here, and that is entirely the point.

New American·$$

Canvas Restaurant & Market

Canvas sits right on the water in Laureate Park, which makes it one of the only true waterfront tables in Orlando, and the neighborhood treats it like its front porch. Come for brunch and the lobster eggs benedict, stay for a Nona Sunset as the light goes down over the lake. There is a little market attached for provisions and gifts, but the real souvenir is that view, doing its slow evening magic.

Art & Open Air

Outside the Lines

Art, trails, and the weird-beautiful stuff a planned city does when it plans for play.

Public Art·Free

Lake Nona Sculpture Garden

Walk through the Wave Hotel lobby and out the back, and a planned community suddenly turns into a world class art collection with no ticket booth. The Sculpture Garden spreads 50,000 square feet of palms and lawn behind the hotel, stocked from the Lewis Collection: Di Modica's Charging Bull, a Botero, a Henry Moore, and JEFRË's mirrored figures catching the sun. It is free, it is quiet, and it should not exist here. It does.

See the district in the guide →

Public Art·Free

The Beacon & Code Wall

Town Center's skyline is a light show waiting for dark. The Beacon rises six stories, a sculptural tower by artist JEFRË that wakes up after sunset with video, music, and light that answers the crowd, while the Code Wall next door wraps a plain parking garage in dichroic glass patterned with binary code, shifting colors as the light moves. It is public art as civic personality: a suburb that decided its parking garage should be beautiful.

The Itinerary

Make a day of it

Morning

Brunch at Canvas, right on the water. The day starts slow in Lake Nona, and nobody here apologizes for it.

Afternoon

Laps on the aqua park and the sixty-foot tower at Nona Adventure Park, then the Sculpture Garden when your arms give out.

Evening

Graze your way through Boxi Park, or go full theater at Bacán. Either way, end in Town Center and watch the Beacon do its thing.
