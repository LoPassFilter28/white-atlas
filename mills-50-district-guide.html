Mills 50

Orlando's most delicious mile: Vietnamese pho, hand-pulled noodles, and dive bars with legendary status.

Scroll the story

Mills 50 does not ease you in. It hits you with steam first: pho broth rolling out of kitchen doors, garlic and star anise hanging over the sidewalk, a bass line leaking out of a bar that has not changed its mind about anything since the nineties. This is the mile Orlando built for itself, immigrant kitchens and dive bars and band rooms stacked shoulder to shoulder, all of it gloriously uninterested in impressing the theme parks.

The Icons

The legends of the strip, from punk rooms to ramen counters.

Pub·$

Will's Pub & Lil Indie's

If Orlando's underground has an address, it is 1042 North Mills. Will's Pub is the room where punk, metal, post-punk and emo bills pack the floor shoulder to shoulder, and where your new favorite band might be opening. In the second room, Lil Indie's keeps it late and loose: funk DJs, jazz improv, folk strings, often no cover. Two rooms, one block, zero pretension. Come curious, leave converted.

Noodles·$$

Domu

Since November 2016, Domu has been the ramen room Orlando talks about. The East End Market original earned a Michelin Bib Gourmand and a Netflix spotlight, and the bowls live up to it. Start with the kimchi butter wings, then the Richie Rich ramen, and give yourself over to the broth. The room is compact, the line is real, and first come first serve keeps the energy honest. Worth every minute of the wait.

Vietnamese·$

Banh Mi Boy

Michelin handed it a Bib Gourmand, Netflix's Somebody Feed Phil came with cameras, but Banh Mi Boy never changed its tune. This Mills Market fixture, serving for over twenty years, bakes baguettes that shatter and sigh the way Saigon street bread should, then loads them with crispy pork belly, roasted garlic mayo and house pate. The playful side shows in the pho French dip, a whole sandwich begging to be dunked in rich broth, and fresh-pressed sugarcane juice that tastes like afternoon in Vietnam. This is Orlando's Vietnamese soul, perfected over decades.

Come Hungry

The noodle mile

Nobody does Asian comfort like this strip: pho, dumplings, sushi, and curry within a few blocks.

Japanese · Udon·$$

Zaru

Zaru does one thing and does it like a craftsman who has never heard of shortcuts. The udon is Sanuki style, made in house from wheat shipped from Japan's Kagawa Prefecture, thick and bouncy and built to carry broth. The Nikutama, warm noodles with seasoned beef and a soft egg, is the bowl people cross town for. Michelin noticed within the first year and handed over a Bib Gourmand. Slurp loudly. It is polite here.

Vietnamese · Pho·$

Pho Vinh

Every great food street has a room like this: no fuss, no mood lighting, just a menu the size of a phone book and broth that has been perfecting itself for years. Pho Vinh is the Mills 50 old guard, the place cooks from fancier kitchens slip into on their day off. Order the big bowl, tear the herbs in by hand, and watch the whole neighborhood cycle through the door.

Malaysian·$$

Mamak Asian Street Food

Mamak is named for the open air food stalls of Malaysia, and it eats like one that got a design budget. The roti canai arrives flaky and golden, made to be torn and dragged through spicy curry gravy until your fingers shine. Char kway teow comes off the wok with real char on the noodles, and the whole room hums like a night market that stayed past its bedtime. Order family style or regret it.

Late Shift

After midnight

Dive bars, late-night eats, and the rooms where the night actually ends.

Dive Bar·$

Wally's Mills Avenue Liquors

Wally's has been pouring on Mills Avenue since 1959, and the room has the confidence of a place that has outlasted every trend that ever drove past it. The drinks are strong, the pours are honest, and nobody is performing for anybody. Belly up next to off shift bartenders and neighborhood lifers, feed the jukebox, and understand that this is not a dive bar costume. This is the real article, still standing.

Asian Latin Fusion·$

Tako Cheena

Tako Cheena is what happens when a kitchen refuses to pick a continent. Korean beef takos, pernil with mojo, empanadas, burritos the size of a forearm, all of it rolling out of a tiny, art covered room on Mills Avenue that feeds the line until deep into the night. This is the post show fuel, the after shift ritual, the place every Will's Pub night eventually points toward.

The Itinerary

Make a day of it

Morning

Start at Mills Market. Banh mi and sugarcane juice for breakfast. This is the way.

Afternoon

Noodle crawl down Colonial: pho at Pho Vinh, roti at Mamak, udon at Zaru. Pace yourself. Nobody does.

Evening

Dinner at Domu, then Will's Pub for the headliner. End at Wally's or Tako Cheena, whichever pulls you first.
