Disney Springs

Disney's grown-up playground: waterfront dining, live music, and shopping that actually earns the hype.

Scroll the story

Here is the local's secret: the best night at Disney World never enters a park. Disney Springs grew up around real Florida springs and a working waterfront, four neighborhoods of chef-driven restaurants, serious bars, and live music on every corner, all of it free to walk into. Come for one dinner and you will plan the next visit before dessert lands.

The Icons

The names that made Springs a dining destination, not just a shopping district.

Bakery·$$

Gideon's Bakehouse (Disney Springs Flagship)

Steve Lewis started baking cookies in his kitchen and ended up with the hardest line in Disney Springs. These are nearly half-pound handmade monsters that take over 24 hours to prepare, and the Original Chocolate Chip alone converted a city of cynics. But Gideon's is bigger than the cookies: a candlelit, Edward Gorey Victorian bakehouse where a gargoyle guards the case and the cold brew is serious business. Go early, go hungry, and don't skip the cake slices.

Restaurant·$$$

The Boathouse & Vintage Amphicars

Every good Disney Springs dinner wants a waterfront, and The Boathouse owns the best one. The raw bar and the steaks both earned their reputations honestly, and the deck at golden hour is the kind of view that slows your table down. But here's the move: book the captain-guided Amphicar tour. It's the only place in the world where a vintage 1960s amphibious car splashes straight off the dock and carries you around the lake.

Wine Bar·$$$

Wine Bar George

George Miliotes, one of a few hundred Master Sommeliers on the planet, built his wine bar like a winemaker's own estate in the middle of Disney Springs, and the whole place glows with his obsession. More than 200 wines pour by the glass, the bottle, or the ounce, so you can taste a legendary vintage without committing to the bottle. Small plates, charcuterie, family style feasts: everything on the table feels chosen for what is in your glass. Wine education without the snobbery.

Chef-Driven Rooms

Dinner, but make it Disney Springs

Chef-driven rooms worth crossing property for.

Restaurant·$$$

Jaleo

Of all the big-league chef outposts that have landed in Orlando, this is the one worth a pilgrimage. José Andrés built his largest Jaleo at Disney Springs, and it delivers Spain with real conviction: jamón sliced so thin it glistens, tapas that taste like a Madrid night out, and wood-fired paellas layered with saffron and garlic aioli that make a group dinner feel like a celebration. Roaming rumba guitarists on Sundays, a sangria hour at the Matador Bar, and Pepe's bocatas next door for the grab-and-go version. This is the rare celebrity restaurant that eats like a love letter.

Restaurant·$$$

Morimoto Asia

Iron Chef Masaharu Morimoto chose Disney Springs for his first pan-Asian restaurant, and he built a cathedral: two stories of glass and steel, chandeliers dripping thirty-six feet from the ceiling, and a kitchen that treats a Peking duck like a ceremony, carved right at your table. The spare ribs arrive lacquered and sticky, the sushi bar works with a surgeon's calm, and the dim sum would hold its own in any Chinatown on earth. Dinner here feels like an event because it is one.

Seafood·$$$

Paddlefish

You are dining on a paddlewheel riverboat, and Paddlefish never lets you forget it in the best way. Three decks of seafood on the water: crab cakes and lobster corn dogs below, whole fish and steaks in the dining room, and a rooftop deck up top where the whole lake turns gold at sunset. The boat has fed Orlando under one name or another for decades, and this version is the sharpest it has ever been. Order the catch, watch the water, stay for one more round.

After Dark

After dark on the waterfront

When the sun drops, Springs turns up: live music spilling from every corner, the water lit up, and nowhere else to be.

Music Venue·$$

House of Blues Orlando

You can see the rusty water tower from across the lake, and it has been the West Side's lighthouse for live music for decades. Inside, the walls wear folk art like scripture and the music hall books everyone from legacy acts to the band your kid swears is next. The restaurant next door does Southern cooking with conviction, and the Sunday Gospel Brunch fills the room with voices that raise the roof before noon. This is Springs at full volume.

Irish Pub·$$

Raglan Road Irish Pub

Raglan Road was built the honest way: bars, woodwork, and antiques shipped over from Ireland and assembled into a pub that feels older than the state it sits in. Every night the stage fills with Irish bands and dancers whose feet move faster than your eyes can follow, and the whole room claps along whether it planned to or not. Fish and chips, shepherd's pie, a proper pour, and a crowd that came for dinner and stayed for the session.

The Itinerary

Make a day of it

Morning

Beat the line at Gideon's, coffee in hand, then walk Town Center while the shops are still quiet and the springs are glass.

Afternoon

Splash off the dock in an Amphicar, browse the Marketplace, then let Wine Bar George pour you something you have never heard of.

Evening

Dinner at Jaleo or on the Paddlefish rooftop, then follow the music: dancers at Raglan Road, a set at House of Blues, the water lit up the whole walk home.
