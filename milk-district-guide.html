Milk District

The Milk District runs on grit and flavor: old-school sandwiches, indie breweries, and bakeries with lines out the door.

Scroll the story

The Milk District got its name the honest way: from the T.G. Lee dairy plant that has anchored Robinson Street for a century, its mural cow still watching the traffic roll past. What grew up around it is Orlando at its grittiest and tastiest, a few walkable blocks of old Florida bones and new ambition where the sandwich counters have waiting lines and the brewers pour fifty feet from the tanks.

The Icons

The heavyweights of the district, the names that built its reputation.

Sandwiches·$

Beefy King

Every city has a sandwich shop its people would defend in a fistfight, and in Orlando that shop is Beefy King. Since 1968, Freeman and Margaret Smith's counter on Bumby Avenue has been steaming roast beef until it falls apart in a good way, piling it onto soft, steamed kaiser rolls with diced onions, and sending it out with Beefy Spuds, crisp tater tots by another name, and chili that tastes like decades of practice. It survived an arson fire and came back swinging. Go for lunch and understand why nobody here touches the drive-thru chain version.

Bakery·$

Se7en Bites

Ask what happened when a spa-industry veteran followed her grandmother's recipes to culinary school and opened a Milk District bake shop, and the answer is Se7en Bites: Guy Fieri on Triple D, a Michelin nod, and a line out the door. The chicken pot pie emerges at 11 and vanishes. The Vanilla Bean Bourbon Bacon Moon Pie is the legend. Their motto says let us fill your pie hole, and the Minnie Pearl chicken and waffles and the Se7enth Heaven smash burger back it up. Come early, they sell out.

Brewery·$$

Sideward Brewing Co.

Their tagline says it best: making great beer and being super humble about it. Sideward is a family-owned brewery set inside a renovated hundred-year-old warehouse in the Milk District, twenty taps pouring fifty feet from where the beer was made. The fruit-forward brews are the calling card, think watermelon and dragonfruit wheat, a smoothie Berliner thick with banana, mango, and pineapple, alongside lagers, IPAs, and a honey wine that surprises everyone. The covered patio, full kitchen, and house-made everything make this the kind of place you meant to stop at for one and stayed for the afternoon.

Graze the Blocks

The food crawl

Come hungry and walk it: this is a district built for grazing, one legendary counter after another.

Cuban · Rum Bar·$$

Otto's High Dive

The name comes from a grandfather who ran a neighborhood bar in Manzanillo, Cuba, back in the island's cocktail golden age, and the grandson built him a worthy tribute on Robinson Street. Otto's High Dive is a whitewashed, plant-draped rum bar pouring daiquiris by the pitcher and a Cuba Libre on tap, with a Michelin Bib Gourmand on the wall. Order the ropa vieja, the grown-up shrimp cocktail, and whatever the bartender is excited about.

Italian Deli · Market·$

Stasio's Italian Deli

Stasio's shares a wall and a parking lot with Sideward Brewing, and between the two of them they own this corner of Bumby Avenue. The namesake sub stacks prosciutto, hot capicola, soppressata, fresh mozzarella, and roasted red peppers on bread baked in house every morning, and it is honestly two meals wearing a trench coat. Order at the meat counter, browse the imported pastas and the cheese case while you wait, and save room for a cannoli.

Sandwiches·$

Bad As's Sandwiches

Chef John Collazo bakes the bread in house, stacks his sandwiches past the point of structural sense, and still keeps something on the menu for about six bucks. Bad As's is the Milk District's maximalist sandwich shop, all big flavors and bigger builds, with names you will have to say out loud at the counter. Everything here is made with love, his words and the kitchen's whole operating system, and you can taste the difference.

After Dark

The pour

Breweries, bars, and late nights: the district drinks as well as it eats.

Pool Hall · Bar·$

Sportstown Billiards

Sportstown has been racking balls on Robinson Street since 1958, back when this was still cow pasture and the upstairs apartment had, let us say, an interesting history. Today it is Orlando's beloved dive pool hall: free tables, shuffleboard, skee-ball, ping pong, a jukebox with opinions, and one of the deepest and cheapest craft beer lists in town, 250 deep by some counts. New owners took over and wisely changed almost nothing.

Food Hall · Cocktails·$$

Orlando Milkhouse

The Milk District's living room sits on Bumby Avenue under a name that honors the dairy that started it all. The Milkhouse gathers local heavyweights under one roof: Foxtail Coffee, Kelly's Homemade Ice Cream, Ravenous Pig Brewing on tap, and a kitchen turning out smashburgers and hot chicken sandwiches. The full bar shakes up mezcal cocktails, the patio is dog friendly, and the whole room hums like a neighborhood that found its couch.

The Itinerary

Make a day of it

Morning

Se7en Bites, early. The chicken pot pie comes out at 11 and the line forms before it does. Grab a Moon Pie for the road.

Afternoon

Stasio's for the sub, Sideward for the pour. They share a wall on Bumby, so the whole crawl is about forty steps.

Evening

Otto's for rum and ropa vieja, then pool at Sportstown until the jukebox runs out of opinions.
