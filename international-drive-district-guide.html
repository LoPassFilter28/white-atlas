International Drive

I-Drive after dark: supper clubs, sky-high wheels, and the most gloriously over-the-top strip in Florida.

Scroll the story

Every city has a street it shows off, and Orlando's glows. International Drive started as the support road for a theme park empire, then refused to stay in its lane: dinner shows, sky wheels, upside-down houses, and enough neon to read by. Locals joke about the traffic and then drive over anyway, because when the sun drops and the strip lights up, nowhere in Florida does a night out bigger.

The Icons

The heavy hitters of the strip.

Park·$$

ICON Park & The Orlando Eye

The whole I-Drive night skyline has a new compass point. ICON Park's 400-foot wheel lifts you above the city for a slow, twenty-minute soar with Orlando spread out in every direction, and the ground below keeps the energy going: SEA LIFE's ocean tunnel, Madame Tussauds, live music at Tin Roof, and SlingShot launching the brave 450 feet straight up. No gates, no ticket to wander in, just a park that keeps giving you reasons to stay.

Latin·$$$

Mango's Tropical Cafe

Ask anyone who came for dinner and stayed till close. Mango's Tropical Cafe took everything it learned on South Beach's Ocean Drive and rebuilt it bigger on International Drive, and the legend grew with the room. Supper-club dining under a waterfall of live Latin music, dancers, and handcrafted cocktails, then the whole building becomes the nightclub. It's the only spot on I-Drive where dinner, a show, and a dance floor were never separate plans.

Attraction·$$

Andretti Indoor Karting and Games

Named for racing legend Mario Andretti, this 150,000-square-foot entertainment palace lives up to the family name. Electric go-karts rocket around multiple indoor tracks at 35 mph, and the rest is just as stacked: a two-level laser tag arena, 120 arcade games, boutique bowling, VR experiences, pro F1 racing simulators, and an extreme ropes course with curved ziplines. The restaurant and full bar keep the fuel going, and the walls carry racing memorabilia that honors the name on the door. Birthday-party headquarters, corporate-event legend, or just a very fast Saturday.

Eatertainment

Dinner and a show

Eatertainment is the local religion: dinner with a side of spectacle.

Dinner Show·$$

Pirates Dinner Adventure

Only on I-Drive would dinner come with a naval battle. Pirates Dinner Adventure floats a full-size 18th-century Spanish galleon in a 300,000-gallon indoor lagoon, then fills it with sword fights, aerial stunts, cannon fire, and a crew of singing renegades led by Captain Sebastian the Black. You eat a three-course feast from your own ship in the surrounding arena, cheer for your pirate, and might get pulled into the action. Subtle? Never. That's the point.

Dinner Show·$

The Outta Control Magic Comedy Dinner Show

Inside the upside-down house at WonderWorks, one man runs the funniest dinner show on the strip. Tony Brent does it all himself: close-up magic, mind reading, impersonations, improv comedy, even a Houdini straightjacket escape, while the kitchen keeps unlimited hand-tossed pizza, salad, and dessert coming. The theater seats about 125, so every seat is close and nobody is safe from becoming part of the act. The Travel Channel named it one of America's best magic shows.

Beyond the Parks

The attractions mile

Beyond the parks: the weird, wild, wonderful stuff that makes I-Drive I-Drive.

Attraction·$$

WonderWorks

You cannot miss it: a whole building landed upside down on its roof, palm trees pointing at the pavement. WonderWorks plays it as Professor Wonder's secret lab, ripped loose by an experiment gone wrong, and inside are more than 100 hands-on exhibits: feel hurricane-force winds, design a roller coaster and ride your own creation, lie on a bed of nails, land a space shuttle. It is science class run by showmen, and the dinner show upstairs keeps the night going.

See it in the guide →

Attraction·$$

Ripley's Believe It or Not!

The building looks like it is sinking into a sinkhole, which is exactly the joke: Florida's most famous odditorium leans like the ground gave up. Inside, sixteen galleries of oddities Robert Ripley collected from every corner of the planet: shrunken heads, a vampire hunting kit, artwork spun from candy, illusions that bend the room around you. It has been an I-Drive rite of passage for generations. Go in skeptical. Come out a believer, or at least grinning.

The Itinerary

Make a day of it

Morning

Start slow. Big breakfast, strong coffee, and a daylight lap down the strip, when you can take in how gloriously extra it all is.

Afternoon

WonderWorks and Ripley's back to back. Oddities, illusions, and air conditioning: the holy trinity of an Orlando afternoon.

Evening

Dinner with pirates, the Wheel after dark, then Mango's until the music says stop. It will not say stop early.
