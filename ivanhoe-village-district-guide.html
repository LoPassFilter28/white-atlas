Ivanhoe Village

Lakeside mornings and brewery afternoons: Ivanhoe Village is Orlando's easygoing creative quarter.

Scroll the story

Nobody stumbles into Ivanhoe Village by accident. You end up here because someone who loves this city grabbed your sleeve and said, trust me, the lake. Then the village does the rest: brunch plates landing at White Wolf, vintage racks and tiny shops up Orange Avenue, a brewery pouring two blocks from the water. It is Orlando with its guard down, the version locals keep for themselves and share only with people they actually like.

The Icons

The rooms the neighborhood runs on.

Cafe·$$

White Wolf Cafe

This is Ivanhoe Village's morning ritual. White Wolf does Southern comfort the way it should be done: the Crab Cakes Benedict that regulars dream about, French toast worth the trip alone, and a cinnamon bun famous across the neighborhood. Cheese grits, home fries, country fried chicken, the whole honest lineup. The room is art-filled and warm, the staff treats you like a neighbor, and brunch here feels like the day already started right.

Brewery·$$

Ivanhoe Park Brewing Company

Tucked into the historic Ivanhoe District, this independent brewery pours its heart into every pint. The story runs deep: the name honors Lake Ivanhoe founder George Russell and the old Joyland amusement park that once stood here. The beer follows suit with personality, from the crisp Park Hopp'r pilsner to the bold Tupac ShaPorter. The reclaimed-wood taproom and dog-friendly beer garden hum with neighborhood energy, and the rotating taps keep locals coming back to see what is new.

Tapas·$$

Santiago's Bodega

Every neighborhood keeps one table where the night properly starts, and on Virginia Drive it is Santiago's. The tapas arrive the way they should, small plate by small plate: patatas bravas with real heat, saganaki still sizzling, lamb chops kissed by the grill. Sangria flows red and white, the patio glows under the palms, and nobody at your table is in a hurry to leave. Order one more plate. Everyone always orders one more plate.

Made By Hand

The Creative Quarter

Galleries, vintage shops, and studios: the village wears its art on its sleeve.

Artist Studios·Free to wander

FAVO · Faith Arts Village Orlando

FAVO took a tired old roadside motel on Colonial Drive and turned its rooms into working art studios, more than twenty-five of them strung around a courtyard. Painters, printmakers, sculptors and mixed media artists make real work behind those motel doors, and on First Friday nights the whole place swings open: doors wide, new work on the walls, neighbors drifting through with drinks in hand. This is the creative quarter with its sleeves rolled up.

Gift Shop·$$

YAY Tiny Shop

Two hundred and fifty square feet. That is the entire store, and somehow it is enough. YAY Tiny Shop packs its white brick corner on Orange Avenue with Jellycat plush, Paddywax candles, cards and small batch goods from local makers, four shoppers at a time, elbow to elbow and grinning. The shelves are stocked with whatever made the owner smile that week, and it shows. You stop in for a birthday card and leave with a bag of small joys for three different people. Tiny is the whole point.

On The Water

Lake Life

Lake Ivanhoe anchors it all: paddleboards, sunset walks, and the calmest water in the city.

Clear Kayaks & Paddleboards·$$

Epic Paddle Adventures

The clear kayaks change everything. Epic Paddle Adventures launches from the Lake Ivanhoe boat ramp and puts you on spring-fed water so transparent you can watch the lake floor slide by under your hull. Paddle the chain of lakes: downtown skyline ahead, quiet coves full of herons behind, grand shoreline homes at the far end. Come back at golden hour for the sunset paddle, or after dark, when the boats light up and glow across the water. Beginners welcome. Dogs too.

Lakeside Park·Free

Gaston Edwards Park

Gaston Edwards Park is the village front porch: a green stretch of Lake Ivanhoe shoreline with a playground, sand volleyball courts, picnic shade and the boat ramp where the paddlers put in. Locals walk the water's edge at golden hour, when the downtown skyline catches the light across the lake and the whole surface turns to copper. Bring a coffee, bring the dog, take the long way around. Nobody on this shoreline is checking a clock.

The Itinerary

Make a day of it

Morning

Brunch at White Wolf, then a slow walk down to the lake while the water is still glass.

Afternoon

Clear kayak on Lake Ivanhoe, then studio doors and vintage racks: FAVO, YAY, and every little shop on Orange Avenue.

Evening

Tapas at Santiago's as the sky goes lavender, then a pint at Ivanhoe Park Brewing. The neighborhood, start to finish.
