407 FYI · District Highlights

Winter Park

Brick streets, a canopy of hundred-year oaks, and the prettiest avenue in Florida. This is Orlando's old soul, and it has never tried to be anything else.

Scroll the story

Winter Park doesn't do theme parks. It does morning light through live oaks, coffee on Park Avenue, and lakes so still they double the sky. Founded as a winter retreat in the 1880s, it kept the manners and lost the stuffiness. Today it is gallery strolls, farmers markets, and some of the best eating in Central Florida, all under that famous canopy.

The Icons

Every district has its legends. These are the names locals say with their chests.

Brewery·$$$

The Ravenous Pig & Brewing Co.

Ask a Winter Park local where to eat and watch their face change when they say this name. Husband-and-wife chefs James and Julie Petrakis turned a warm, butcher-shop-beautiful gastropub into the restaurant that taught Orlando to take its own food seriously. The pig board alone is worth crossing town for, the shrimp 'n grits taste like the Gulf showing off, and the room hums with the particular joy of a place that has never phoned it in.

Park·$$

Winter Park Scenic Boat Tour

There is no better introduction to Winter Park than a seat on one of these little pontoon boats. Since 1938, the Scenic Boat Tour has been gliding through the chain of lakes and the narrow man-made canals that stitch them together, past Rollins College and waterfront mansions with real stories in their bones. Your skipper narrates the whole hour, and the ride has a way of slowing your breathing down. Bring your out-of-town guests here first. It reframes everything else.

Bakery·$$

The Glass Knife

Walk in and look at the walls: antique glass cake knives, the unlikely 1920s invention that gave this place its name. The Glass Knife is a French cafe that grew up in Florida, and everything here shows intention. The creme brulee donut, made from 24-hour buttermilk brioche, has a following of its own, and the coffee is Onyx, exclusively, pulled fresh every time. Weekend brunch is full table service; weekdays you order at the counter and linger on the covered patio.

The Main Street

Park Avenue

Park Avenue is the runway. Brick-paved, fountain-anchored, lined with boutiques and wine bars and people who look like they brunch professionally. Come to browse, stay because the light is perfect.

Historic District·Free to stroll

Park Avenue Historic District

Stand on the brick pavers at golden hour and you understand why nobody here is in a hurry. Park Avenue runs ten blocks of boutiques, wine bars, art galleries and sidewalk cafes, with Central Park's green lawn and fountain rolling out across the street. Window shop at Rifle Paper Co., get lost in the Writer's Block Bookstore, then claim a cafe table and watch the whole town parade past in the oak shade.

Sushi · Omakase·$$$$

Kadence

Nine seats. That is the whole restaurant, and every one of them faces the knives. Kadence is a Michelin starred omakase counter where chefs Mark Berdin, Jennifer Bañagale and Lordfer Lalicon work in near silence, handing you nigiri piece by piece: Hokkaido uni, fatty otoro, fish flown in and treated like scripture. There is no menu to hide behind. You surrender, course after course, and it is the best decision you will make all year.

Italian·$$

Prato

Prato does Italian the way Winter Park does everything: beautifully, and like it was always meant to be here. The wood fired oven blisters pizzas under the glow of the long bar, handmade pastas land glossy and rich, and the mustard spaghettini cacio e pepe has converted more skeptics than any dish in town. Grab a patio table facing the avenue, order the Widowmaker, and let lunch become dinner.

Culture, Quietly Stacked

The Quiet Flex

The quiet flex: Winter Park holds the world's most comprehensive collection of Tiffany glass, a Mediterranean-style sculpture garden, and a 1930s Spanish farmhouse that throws the prettiest parties in town.

Art Museum·$

Charles Hosmer Morse Museum of American Art

You walk in expecting a nice little museum and walk out converted. The Morse holds the world's most comprehensive collection of Louis Comfort Tiffany: lamps that bloom like flowers, windows that hold light hostage, a jewel box chapel from the 1893 Chicago World's Fair, and whole rooms rescued from his Laurelton Hall estate. Stand in the Daffodil Terrace as the glass glows around you and try not to whisper. Everyone whispers.

Museum & Gardens·$

Albin Polasek Museum & Sculpture Gardens

Czech sculptor Albin Polasek built his Mediterranean style home and studio on three acres of Lake Osceola shoreline, and the gardens still feel like his private conversation with the water. Bronze figures rise out of the greenery, a small chapel hides in the shade, and the Capen House next door arrived by barge after the town refused to let it be demolished. Come for the sculpture. Stay for the quiet.

Historic Home·Free

Casa Feliz Historic Home Museum

Casa Feliz means happy house, and the name is not marketing. Architect James Gamble Rogers II built this Andalusian farmhouse in 1933 with whitewashed brick, hand made Barcelona roof tiles, a bell tower and a courtyard built for long evenings. When demolition threatened, Winter Park picked the whole house up and moved it rather than say goodbye. Now it hosts the prettiest weddings and parties in town, under the oaks at the top of Park Avenue.

The Itinerary

Make a day of it

Morning

Coffee and the avenue. Watch the town wake up.

Afternoon

Morse Museum, then the boat tour. Yes, both. Thank me later.

Evening

Dinner at the Pig. If you only do one thing, do this.
