Banh Mi Boy

Noodles & Southeast Asian · $

1110 E Colonial Dr, Orlando, FL 32803

Michelin handed it a Bib Gourmand and Netflix's Somebody Feed Phil came with cameras, but Banh Mi Boy never changed its tune. A Mills Market fixture serving for over twenty years, it bakes baguettes that shatter the way Saigon street bread should, then loads them with crispy pork belly, roasted garlic mayo and house pate. The playful side shows in the pho French dip, a sandwich built for dunking in rich broth, and fresh-pressed sugarcane juice. For Vietnamese sandwiches in Mills 50, this is the benchmark regulars measure others against.

Banh Mi Cali

Noodles & Southeast Asian · $

Banh Mi Cali, 5210 W Colonial Dr, Orlando, FL 32808-7606

No frills, no pretense, just one of Orlando's most honest banh mi counters in Mills 50. The sandwiches come on crisp, airy baguettes stuffed with the classics: roast pork, meatballs, house pate and terrine, with pickled carrot and daikon crunching through every bite. Regulars round it out with a strong Vietnamese iced coffee or a milk tea, and the doughnuts earn their own quiet reputation. Cowboy Curry

From the UniGirl family comes a love letter to Japanese curry done the slow way: forty hours, forty ingredients, and a sauce sweetened only with fruits and vegetables, no gluten, no dairy, just depth. Edoboy

Japanese & sushi · $$

728 N Thornton Ave, Orlando, FL 32803

Eight seats, no chairs, and twelve pieces at a standing counter: Edoboy is Orlando's first standing sushi bar. Chef Sean "Sonny" Nguyen built the passion project after travels through Tokyo, Osaka, and Kyoto, where standing sushi is a local shortcut to excellence. You book a slot on Resy, stand at the counter, and order nigiri and hand rolls from the chefs piece by piece, each one brushed and ready. Finish with miso soup made from the fish bones of the day and, wisely, the matcha s'more. Fast, fun, and fiercely good, it rewards planning.
