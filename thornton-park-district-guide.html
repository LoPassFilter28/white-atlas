The District

Brick Streets, Front Porch

Two blocks from the downtown towers, streets turn to brick and porches come out to greet you. Thornton Park is Orlando's front porch: bungalows under old oaks, a fountain on Washington Street, Lake Eola glittering at every block's end.

Southern, Finished Like Art

Soco is Southern cooking that went to finishing school: Greg Richie plates grilled meatloaf on lobster mashed potatoes under smoked onion gravy, and potato-chip chocolate cake with salted caramel to fight over. The patio watches Central Boulevard roll by.

Flashback · Orlando History

What happened to the Lake Eola swans?

They were rehomed in 2026. After an avian flu outbreak killed more than 30 birds, the city ended the century-old program and moved the remaining 44 swans ahead of a $60 million park renovation, so if the lake looks quieter, that's why.

The flock started with soap-opera drama: in 1910 an Englishman bought four swans for Lake Lucerne, including a royal mute swan named Billy (supposedly from King George's own Thames flock) who drowned his mate, chased cars, and nipped schoolchildren. After Billy feuded with the lake's black swans, a pair was moved to Lake Eola in 1922 and founded the flock.

Local note: downtown's most famous residents were British royalty with anger issues.
