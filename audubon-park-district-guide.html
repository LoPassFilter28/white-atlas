Audubon Park

The Garden District: a food hall that launched legends, cupcake royalty, and the greenest streets in Orlando.

Scroll the story

Orlando has louder neighborhoods, but none greener. Audubon Park grew up around gardens and good appetites: bungalow streets shaded by old oaks, a market hall where bakers and cheesemongers set up shop side by side, and a main drag, Corrine Drive, that still moves at walking pace. People come here to eat slowly, shop small, and remember what a neighborhood is supposed to feel like.

The Icons

The rooms that put the Garden District on the map.

Noodles·$$

Domu

Since November 2016, Domu has been the ramen room Orlando talks about. The East End Market original earned a Michelin Bib Gourmand and a Netflix spotlight, and the bowls live up to it. Start with the kimchi butter wings, then the Richie Rich ramen, and give yourself over to the broth. The room is compact, the line is real, and first come first serve keeps the energy honest. Worth every minute of the wait.

Coffee Shop·$

East End Market

Forget the food courts you know. East End Market is a culinary hub in Audubon Park where a market hall, incubator kitchens, and an event space all hum under one roof. Line up for the famous Gideon's Bakehouse cookies, tear into a loaf from Olde Hearth Bread, sip Lineage Coffee, and wander past La Femme du Fromage and Domu. Every corner belongs to a local maker with something to prove. Come hungry, leave with a bag full of somebody's craft.

Bakery·$

Blue Bird Bake Shop

Audubon Park's cupcake institution has been nesting on Corrine Drive for years, spinning more than sixty cupcake flavors through a daily rotation: red velvet, Neapolitan, tuxedo, peanut butter and jelly, holiday specials that locals plan around. Everything baked from scratch, the cases often selling out by afternoon. It doubles as a quirky morning hangout, coffee and a scone under hexagon tile and damask wallpaper. This is the bakery the neighborhood measures other bakeries against.

Inside the Hall

The market hall effect

One building, a dozen makers: how East End Market turned a neighborhood into a food destination.

Bakery·East End Market Original

Gideon's Bakehouse

Before the Disney Springs lines, there was a counter the size of a closet inside East End Market. Steve Lewis started Gideon's here in 2016 with a family chocolate chip recipe, a stack of vintage books, and cookies that weigh half a pound each. The market gave him room to be strange and excellent at the same time, and Orlando rewarded him with a line out the door. Legends have humble first addresses. This is his.

Coffee Roaster·East End Market

Lineage Coffee Roasting

Lineage started with a roaster, a stubborn streak, and a belief that Orlando deserved coffee treated like produce: sourced carefully, roasted in small batches, poured without a fuss. Their East End Market bar became the district's morning meeting point, all bright cups and regulars who know each other's orders. Order the single origin pour over, watch the market wake up around you, and try to leave without a bag of beans. Nobody manages it.

The Neighborhood

Garden District living

Bungalows, birds, and the Sunday market: the softest landing in Orlando.

Botanical Garden·50 Acres

Harry P. Leu Gardens

Fifty acres of camellias, roses, and old Florida green, and it sits right at the district's edge like Audubon Park's own backyard. Harry P. Leu Gardens rolls from manicured flower beds to bamboo groves and a butterfly garden, with the historic Leu House standing quiet in the middle of it all. Walk it slow after breakfast. The egrets will not hurry, and neither should you.

See the district in the guide →

Community Market·Corrine Drive

Audubon Park Community Market

One evening a week, the lot beside Stardust Video and Coffee fills with tents, food trucks, and half the neighborhood. The Audubon Park Community Market is the district's living room: local farmers, bakers, makers, and musicians, kids on bikes, dogs working the crowd for samples. Grab dinner from a truck, find the honey stand, and let the garden district show you how it feeds itself.

The Itinerary

Make a day of it

Morning

Blue Bird first: a cupcake and coffee on Corrine Drive, then a slow loop through Leu Gardens while the light is still soft.

Afternoon

East End Market, unhurried. A loaf, a wedge of cheese, a cookie the size of your hand, and nowhere else to be.

Evening

Back to the market hall for Domu. Join the line, order the wings and the Richie Rich, and let the garden go quiet around you.
